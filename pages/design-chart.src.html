Principles

Five rules that decide the rest

Every specific rule further down is downstream of one of these. When a new case appears that the chart does not cover, resolve it against these rather than inventing a sixth.

1

Summary before detail

A view opens with what would make someone act — the count, the state, the thing that is overdue — and only then the rows. An operator should be able to close the tab after two seconds and know whether anything needs them.

2

Colour never carries meaning alone

Every status has a hue, a shape and a word. The interface has to survive greyscale, colour blindness, and a cheap monitor in a workshop with the door open.

3

One primary action per view

Exactly one filled button. If two things look equally primary, the user has to make a decision the design should have made for them.

4

Elevation is rare, so it means something

Cards are flat: a hairline and the page ground. Shadow is reserved for things that genuinely float — dialogs and menus. A page where everything is raised has no hierarchy at all.

5

Name things the way the user does

A person manages people and workshops, not principals and tenants. The database schema is not the vocabulary.

Layout

One container, one spacing scale

Content sits in a single centred column capped at 72rem. There is no multi-column page grid: the views are lists and forms, and a column system would be scaffolding holding up nothing.

Navigation

Two levels, never three

The global bar answers which part of the product. Tabs answer which view of this thing. Anything that wants to be a third level is a page reached from a link in the content, not another row of chrome.

Density

Interface scale, not marketing scale

The product runs at 15 px body and 13 px in tables and controls. That is smaller than the landing page on purpose: a marketing page is read once, a members list is scanned a hundred times, and generous type turns scanning into scrolling.

Motion

Two durations, one curve

Motion confirms that something happened; it is not decoration and it never delays. An operator waiting on a five-minute provisioning job should feel the work, not the interface.

The whole vocabulary
--mb-dur-fast   120ms   hover, press, focus --mb-dur-slow   200ms   progress, reveal, layout --mb-ease       cubic-bezier(.2,0,0,1)

One easing curve for everything. Mixed curves in one interface read as two products stitched together.

What is allowed to move
Do Colour and border on hover; the progress bar's width; a card lifting 1 px. Each confirms a state change the user caused or is waiting on.
Don't Entrance animations on lists, staggered fades, anything on page load. The content was already there. Animating its arrival only postpones reading it.

prefers-reduced-motion reduces every duration to 0.01ms, including the spinner.

Accessibility

Non-negotiable, and cheap if done first
A

One focus treatment, never removed

A 2 px accent outline at 2 px offset, on every focusable thing. :focus-visible keeps it off mouse clicks, so there is no reason left to delete it. Tab into the buttons above to see it.

B

Contrast is measured, not eyeballed

Body text clears AAA in both themes; every status colour clears AA as text; control borders clear 3:1. Two palette values were moved off their natural ramp to get there rather than being shipped as near-misses.

C

Status is hue, shape and word

Every badge has a dot and a label. Desaturate this page and nothing becomes ambiguous.

D

Errors are announced, not just shown

Error text carries role="alert"; invalid fields carry aria-invalid and point at their message with aria-describedby. A red border alone tells a screen reader nothing.

E

Targets stay at 40 px

Buttons and inputs have a 2.5 rem minimum height. Workshop screens get used with clay on your hands and a phone propped against a wheel.

Writing

The interface is mostly words

Copy is designed, not filled in afterwards. Three rules cover almost every case: name the action, say what actually happened, and never apologise instead of explaining.

Controls say what they do
DoCreate and provisionThen a confirmation that says Workshop created. The label and the result use the same word.
Don'tSubmitNames the mechanism, not the outcome. Nobody came here to submit anything.
Errors explain and recover
DoWe could not reach the identity service, so the invitation was not sent. Nothing else changed. Try again in a moment.What failed, what it means for their data, what to do next.
Don'tError: request failed (500)True and useless. The status code belongs in the logs, not in front of a potter.
Empty states offer the next step
