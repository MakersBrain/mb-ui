Candidate marks

Each shown on paper and on ink, then at 32 / 20 / 16 px and in single colour

All five survived a favicon test and a monochrome test; the ones that did not are not here. The two M letterforms give the strongest name recall, the chop and the weave give the strongest standalone symbol, and the kiln gives the most warmth at the cost of naming one craft.

1 colour
Contained letterform

The chop

The maker's stamp pressed into the base of finished work — the one object every craft shares, and literally a mark. Corner radii are deliberately unequal, so it reads hand-cut rather than app-icon.

TradeStrongest as a favicon and an avatar; weakest as a standalone symbol, because the container does most of the work.
1 colour
Abstract geometric

The weave

Two strands crossing over and under. It is a half-lap joint to a woodworker, a warp and weft to a weaver, and a coil to a basket maker — one geometry that does not pick a craft.

TradeThe only candidate that says something about the software as well as the workshop: two things fitting together. Reads as a frame at a glance; the over-under needs a beat to land.

Letterform + metaphor

The trestle M

An M built as a workbench: two clay posts with a dark brace let into them. The brace passes behind the posts rather than meeting them, which is how the joint would actually be cut.

TradeBest name recall of the set and unmistakable at 16 px. An M in two colours is also the most crowded space in software branding.

Letterform

The folded M

One sheet creased down the middle, the two halves catching different light. Solid rather than stroked, so it holds its weight beside a heavy slab wordmark.

TradeThe most confident silhouette here and the cheapest to keep coherent. Also the least it says: without the colour split it is just an M.

Symbolic object

The kiln

An arch with two pieces on the shelf. The warmest mark of the five and the only one that shows work in progress rather than work finished.

TradeContradicts the "artisans broadly" brief — a kiln is a ceramics kiln. Keep it only if ceramics stays the front door.
Chosen: the weave. The other four are kept here as the record of what was considered and why, not as live options. The system built on it is in the design chart; the assets are in brand/logo/.
Why: the weave. It is the only mark that carries the product's actual idea — craft and record fitting together — without naming a craft, and it is the one that will still be right when the second and third verticals arrive. The trestle M is the safe pick if name recall matters more than meaning.

Typography

Bitter · IBM Plex Sans · JetBrains Mono
Display Bitter

A screen-native slab. It reads as workshop signage, crate stencils and tool catalogues rather than boutique editorial — which is the whole difference between this brand and the cream-and-serif direction every craft company lands on. It also holds its weight down at 18 px, where a high-contrast display serif falls apart.

Interface IBM Plex Sans

Humanist enough to sit beside a slab without arguing with it, and engineered enough for dense tables. Chosen over Inter for its numerals and its Latin-ext coverage, which an ERP running French micro-entreprise paperwork actually exercises.

Data JetBrains Mono

Lot codes, SKUs, QR payloads, SIRET numbers, firing schedules. Anything a person will read back aloud or type into a label printer belongs in mono, where 0 and O cannot be confused.

Rollout

In dependency order — each step needs the one before it

These are genuinely sequential: the mark decides the favicon and the lockup, the lockup decides the shells, and the Odoo overrides are last because they are the only ones that have to fight an upstream theme.

1

Choose the mark

One of the five. Everything downstream is blocked on it, and nothing downstream is expensive once it is settled.

brand/logo/*.svg
2

Finish the chosen mark's asset set

Dark variants with the accent stepped up to clay-400, an outlined-text wordmark that needs no font, favicon and PWA sizes, and a monochrome cut for label printing — the Phomemo path is one-bit, so the mark has to survive with no greys at all.

brand/logo/ — light, dark, mono, wordmark, favicon
3

Retrofit the control plane

Replace the ad-hoc palette in app.css — the deep green on cream, Georgia headings — with the token file. The existing green survives as the success tone, so the diff is smaller than it looks.

control-plane/web/src/app.css, src/routes/+layout.svelte
4

Build the landing page

New surface, no legacy to fight. This is where the display scale and the editorial register earn their keep.

new
5

Odoo backend overrides

Last, and deliberately shallow: brand the login, the app switcher, the label editor and the capture screens, and leave the rest of the Odoo web client alone. Fighting an upstream theme wholesale is a cost that never stops.
